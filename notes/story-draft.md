## Ilé-Ọ̀yọ́

**Ilé-Ọ̀yọ́** means *home of Ọ̀yọ́*.

Ọ̀yọ́ is a Yoruba city, and the heart of the old Ọ̀yọ́ empire. [Author: a
sentence or two on what Ọ̀yọ́ means to you, and why the theme carries its name.]

A color theme is something you look at all day. We wanted this one to feel
like home: warm, grounded, and made to be lived in.

## Woven from aṣọ-òkè

The palette comes from *aṣọ-òkè*, also called *aṣọ òfì*: the hand-loomed
cloth of Ọ̀yọ́'s *agbádá* and *ìró àti bùbá*. It is woven in narrow strips on
a loom and sewn together edge to edge. Ìsẹ̀yìn, in Ọ̀yọ́ State, is known for its
weavers.

Three classic aṣọ-òkè cloths give the theme its core colors:

- **Ẹtù**: a deep indigo, almost black. It's the background of the dark
  variant and the text of the light one.
- **Sányán**: the warm beige of wild silk. It's the text of the dark variant
  and the background of the light one.
- **Àlàárì**: crimson. It's the theme's red, and the accent that marks what's
  active.

Gold thread and the earth tones of clay fill in the rest. The strip pattern
at the top of the site is a nod to how the cloth is made.

## Evening and afternoon

The theme comes in two variants, named for times of day:

- **Alẹ́** (*evening*): the dark variant. Sányán text on ẹtù indigo.
- **Ọ̀sán** (*afternoon*): the light variant. Ẹtù text on sányán.

The two mirror each other, like the same cloth seen by lamplight and by
daylight.

## Made to be read

Beauty shouldn't cost readability. Every text color in Ilé-Ọ̀yọ́ reaches at
least 4.5:1 contrast against its background, the WCAG 2.1 AA standard, and
the build checks this every time the palette changes.
